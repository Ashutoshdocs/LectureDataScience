Part 4 · History of AI

How we got here: 80 years of AI

From a 1943 paper describing a fake neuron, to chatbots used by hundreds of millions, to AI agents that do real work. AI's story has big dreams, two harsh “winters”, and one old idea that kept coming back: neural networks.

Each block is an era. Click one to jump to it in the timeline. Grey blocks are the AI winters. (Widths are not to scale.)
Step 1 · The big pattern

Hype, winter, comeback, repeat

AI has gone through cycles: excitement and funding, then promises that didn't come true, then cuts (“AI winters”). Each comeback was powered by something new: better algorithms, more data, faster computers.

Step 2 · Five big shifts

Who supplies the “intelligence”?

The clearest way to see AI's history: each era moved more of the work from humans to the machine. This ties together everything from Parts 1–3.

1950s–80s

Symbolic AI

Humans write every rule: “IF fever AND rash THEN measles”.

👤 Human writes the rules
1990s–2000s

Machine Learning

Machine learns rules from data, but humans pick the features.

👤 Human picks the features
2012–

Deep Learning

Neural nets learn the features themselves from raw pixels, sound and text.

🤖 Machine learns the features
2018–

Foundation models

One giant model (an LLM) learns from huge amounts of text and does many tasks.

🤖 One model, many tasks
2024–

Agents

Models plan and use tools to act in the world, not just answer.

🤖 Machine takes actions

Step 4 · Why did it work this time?

The three fuels: data, compute, algorithms

Neural networks were invented in the 1940s–50s. They only took off after 2012, because all three fuels finally arrived together.

📚

Data

The internet, smartphones and cameras created oceans of text, photos and video. ImageNet (2009) gave researchers millions of labelled images; LLMs later learned from a large share of the public web.

⚡

Compute

GPUs, built for video games, turned out to be perfect for the matrix maths in neural nets. AlexNet (2012) was trained on two gaming GPUs. Today's models train on tens of thousands.

🧩

Algorithms

Backpropagation (1986), CNNs (1989), LSTMs (1997), then the Transformer (2017) made training deep networks practical and let them scale.

Step 5 · What history teaches

Six lessons from 80 years of AI

1

Old ideas come back

The artificial neuron (1943) was ignored, revived (1986), ignored again, then conquered everything (2012). Good ideas can wait decades for the right hardware.

2

Over-promising causes winters

Researchers in the 1960s predicted human-level AI within a generation. When it didn't happen, funding collapsed. Healthy scepticism is part of the field.

3

Learning beats hand-coding

Again and again, methods that learn from data and scale with compute beat clever hand-written rules. Researcher Rich Sutton called this “The Bitter Lesson” (2019).

4

Games mark the milestones

Checkers (1959), chess (1997), Jeopardy! (2011), Go (2016). Games gave clear, public scoreboards for progress.

5

Usefulness creates booms

Expert systems boomed because XCON saved real money; ChatGPT exploded because anyone could use it. Adoption follows practical value.

6

Rules follow capability

Laws and global agreements (EU AI Act, the AI summits from Bletchley to New Delhi) arrived only after AI became powerful and widespread.
