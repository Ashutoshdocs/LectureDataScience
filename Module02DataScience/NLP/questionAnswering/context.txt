The Taj Mahal is a white marble mausoleum located in Agra, a city in the Indian state of Uttar Pradesh. It stands on the southern bank of the Yamuna river. The Mughal emperor Shah Jahan commissioned the Taj Mahal in 1632 to house the tomb of his favourite wife, Mumtaz Mahal, who died in 1631 while giving birth to their fourteenth child.

The chief architect of the project is generally believed to be Ustad Ahmad Lahauri. Construction of the main tomb was completed around 1643, while work on the surrounding gardens, mosque and other buildings continued until about 1653. Around 20,000 artisans and craftsmen worked on the monument. The white marble was brought from Makrana in Rajasthan, and more than 1,000 elephants are said to have been used to transport building materials.

The central dome rises to a height of about 73 metres. The tomb is surrounded by four minarets, which lean slightly outwards so that they would fall away from the main tomb in the event of an earthquake. Verses from the Quran are inlaid in black marble around the arched entrances.

The Taj Mahal was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983. Today it attracts between 7 and 8 million visitors every year, making it one of the most visited monuments in India. Air pollution has caused the marble to turn yellow over time, so the government has restricted vehicles and factories near the site to protect it.
