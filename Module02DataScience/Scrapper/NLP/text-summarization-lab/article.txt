Plastic pollution has become one of the most serious environmental problems of our time. Every year, millions of tonnes of plastic waste end up in rivers and oceans. Much of this plastic comes from single-use items such as bags, bottles and food packaging. Once in the ocean, plastic breaks down into tiny pieces called microplastics. These microplastics are eaten by fish and other sea creatures, and they can eventually enter the human food chain. Scientists have even found microplastics in drinking water and in the air we breathe.

Governments around the world are starting to take action. Several countries, including India, have banned certain single-use plastic items. Some cities charge customers a small fee for plastic bags, which has sharply reduced their use. Companies are also experimenting with packaging made from paper, glass and plant-based materials.

Recycling helps, but it is not enough on its own. Only a small fraction of all plastic ever made has been recycled, because many types of plastic are difficult or expensive to process. Experts say the most effective solution is to reduce the amount of plastic we use in the first place. Simple habits, such as carrying a reusable bag and water bottle, can make a real difference when millions of people adopt them.
