AI, Machine Learning & Deep Learning

They're not three rival technologies. They are circles inside circles. Every deep learning model is a machine learning model, and every machine learning model is a kind of AI, but not the other way around.

Tap a circle to see what lives in it.

AI: the whole field of making machines do things that seem intelligent. Includes hand-written rules and learning systems.
Step 1 · Definitions

What each one actually means

Think of vehicles: AI is "transport", ML is "motor vehicles", and DL is a high-powered sports car. Same family, very different engines.

Biggest circle

Artificial Intelligence

Any technique that makes a computer act intelligently: decide, plan, recognise, converse.

  • Can be rule-based ("if speed < 10 km/h, it's a jam")
  • Or learned from data (that's ML)
Examples: chess engines with search rules, expert systems, GPS shortest-path routing, chatbots.
Inside AI

Machine Learning

AI that learns patterns from data instead of being told every rule.

  • A human usually picks the features (the useful columns)
  • Algorithms: decision trees, random forest, gradient boosting, linear/logistic regression, SVM
Examples: spam filters, loan approval, house-price prediction, predicting traffic delay from speed + time + rain flag.
Inside ML

Deep Learning

ML using deep neural networks (many layers) that learn the features by themselves from raw data.

  • Feed raw pixels, sound or text directly
  • Architectures: CNNs (images), RNNs/Transformers (sequences, language)
Examples: face unlock, voice assistants, ChatGPT/Claude, self-driving camera perception, medical scan reading.
Common mix-up: not all AI learns. A hand-coded rule like "if the road is red on the map, avoid it" is AI but not ML. It becomes ML when the system learns what "red" should mean from past trips.
Step 2 · The key idea

ML has two separate jobs. DL merges them into one.

This is the most important insight on the page.

Job 1

Feature engineering (representation)

A human looks at raw data and decides what matters. For a handwritten digit: "count the loops, detect straight lines". For traffic: "average speed, hour of day, is it raining".

Job 2

Prediction (classification / regression)

An algorithm learns how those chosen features map to the answer: "jam / no jam", "take Route B", "delay = 18 min".

The weakness: in traditional ML, these two jobs don't talk to each other. If the human picks weak features in Job 1, Job 2 can never fix it.

Why this matters: during training, when the network guesses wrong, the error flows backwards through every layer (backpropagation). So the network doesn't just fix its final guess; it rewrites its early layers to look for better features. Job 1 now learns from the mistakes of Job 2. That feedback loop is what lets DL handle vision, speech and language, where no human could hand-write good features.

Step 4 · Your example, refined

Teaching a car to reroute: where ML ends and DL begins

The idea: "ML can teach my car to spot a traffic jam and find another path, but I need DL to also handle weather, time and distance." Close, with one correction:

The real dividing line isn't which variables you add. It's what form the data arrives in. Weather as a number from an API ("rain = 1") is fine for ML. Weather as a camera image of a storm needs DL.
Inputs: clean numbers

Use ML when…

you can boil traffic, weather, time and distance down to a neat table of numbers: speed 15 km/h, rain = yes, 17:30, Route B = 12 km.

Use DL when…

the car must perceive: see rain on the windscreen, glare on wet asphalt, fog, hear hydroplaning, read free-text driver alerts, and turn that straight into a driving decision.
